06Research

In preparationWorking paper

A Monte Carlo Evaluation of Bootstrap Confidence Interval Coverage

I ran 840,000 simulations to compare four ways of building a confidence interval: Percentile, BCa, Studentized bootstrap and the classical t-interval. I tested them across six distributions, four sample sizes, and both the mean and the median.

  • On heavy-tailed data the intervals miss far more often than they should. For Pareto data at n = 20, a 95% interval covered the true value only 76.4% of the time.
  • The median holds up where the mean fails.
  • BCa was the most stable bootstrap method across settings.

Why it matters here: insurance claim sizes are heavy-tailed. This is the closest my work has come to an actuarial question.
